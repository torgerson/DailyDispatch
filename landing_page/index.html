Markdown and tables

Write like a power user without giving up structure.

Markdown is the source of truth for text. Headings, lists, code, quotes, links, and task syntax stay portable. Tables are there when work needs a real grid instead of prose pretending to be organized.

  • Use slash commands like /table, /task, /date, and /template.
  • Download individual notes as Markdown when you want to move them elsewhere.
  • Keep meeting artifacts, status notes, and decision logs in readable form.

Smart Lists

Saved searches live in the sidebar and inside your notes.

Smart Lists are reusable filters for the way you actually think: blockers, decisions, people, tags, open questions, launch work, or anything else you keep coming back to. Pin them in the sidebar for navigation, or embed them directly inside a note when a project brief needs live context.

  • Sidebar Smart Lists act like always-updating sections.
  • Note Smart Lists turn a static doc into a live dashboard.
  • Use queries like #blocker, @alex, #decision, or combined filters.

Backup and restore

Local-first only works if leaving is easy.

Settings make data ownership explicit. Export a full JSON backup, restore from a previous backup, and download individual notes as Markdown. Your journal should be easy to protect, move, inspect, and walk away with.

  • Back up entries, notes, tasks, Smart Lists, tags, themes, and settings.
  • Restore from JSON if you change browsers or machines.
  • No cloud sync requirement, authentication, or hidden account state.

Feature breakdown

Small enough to understand. Useful enough to keep open all day.

The point is not to replace every tool. The point is to give your thinking a dependable local home with just enough structure to stay searchable later.

Feed-first journal

Every dispatch lands in a chronological feed so your work has a memory. It is less brittle than folders and easier to review than a pile of loose files.

Smart Lists everywhere

Create focused views for #client, @person, open questions, or decisions. Keep them in the sidebar, or embed them inside notes as live context.

Command palette

Find notes, jump around, and execute common actions quickly. The app is designed for people who already think with a keyboard.

Slash commands

Insert tables, tasks, dates, status markers, code blocks, templates, and Smart Lists without hunting through menus.

Task board

Tasks can live inside notes and also roll up into Kanban, calendar, and list views when you need execution mode.

Portable data

Back up JSON, download Markdown, keep copies in git or iCloud, and avoid the quiet lock-in of cloud-only note systems.

Who it is for

You have tried the alternatives.

You liked Obsidian's local files but did not want to design a whole operating system for your notes. You liked Notion's structure but not the cloud dependency. You liked Apple Notes until your work needed better search, markdown, and task context.

DailyDispatch is for engineers, PMs, designers, researchers, consultants, founders, and other people whose work is mostly thinking and typing. It gives you a working system on day one, then stays simple enough that you can understand, modify, and back up the whole thing.

By design

What you do not get.

Every note app wants something from you. DailyDispatch tries very hard not to.

  • No account to create
  • No subscription to manage
  • No cloud sync to configure
  • No Electron app to install
  • No build step or dependencies
  • No terms of service over your journal
  • No one reading your notes but you
  • No data lock-in, ever
